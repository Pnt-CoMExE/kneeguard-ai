Part 1 · Problem identification

Problem background

Why knee MRI interpretation needs AI support.

A single knee MRI scan can reveal a dozen concurrent problems — ligament tears, meniscal damage, three compartments of osteoarthritis, joint effusion, synovitis, Baker's cyst, bone contusion, and fracture. Interpretation quality varies across sites, and access to specialty-trained musculoskeletal radiologists is uneven across healthcare settings.

The RSNA 2026 Knee Abnormality Detection challenge tasks teams to build multimodal models that estimate per-study probabilities for twelve findings, using imaging paired with original multilingual radiology reports during training.

Decision statement: The proposed model will help radiologists and triage teams classify clinically important knee abnormalities by using multimodal knee MRI series and (training-time) radiology report text.

PLACEHOLDER Evidence citations, local statistics, and research notes will be inserted by the team.

Why it matters

  • Single exams may contain multiple concurrent abnormalities requiring simultaneous detection.
  • MSK radiologist expertise is unevenly distributed across sites and regions.
  • Consistent AI triage can reduce delays for high-risk findings such as fracture or ACL disruption.
  • Multimodal training with reports can extend labeling coverage beyond fully annotated studies.
  • Models must generalise across 19+ sites, multiple languages, and diverse scanner protocols.

Project objectives

Two to four measurable objectives — update after team research lock-in.

  • Examine relationships between MRI study characteristics and the twelve labelled abnormalities.
  • Develop a multilabel model that estimates per-finding probabilities for each knee MRI study.
  • Translate model outputs into triage decision rules for radiologist review prioritisation.
  • PLACEHOLDER — Add teammate-specific objective (e.g., report-text weak supervision, efficiency track).

Project questions

  • Which imaging or derived features are most relevant to each of the twelve findings?
  • What patterns appear across sites, scanners, and report languages?
  • Which model family best supports multilabel detection under competition constraints?
  • How should probabilities map to triage actions under human oversight?
  • How will success be measured beyond the leaderboard score?
