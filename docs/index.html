Problem background

Part 1 · Problem identification and justification

Knee MRI can reveal multiple injuries in a single exam—ligament tears, meniscal damage, osteoarthritis, effusion, synovitis, Baker’s cyst, bone contusion, and fracture. Interpretation quality varies, and access to specialty-trained musculoskeletal radiologists is limited in many settings.

The RSNA 2026 Knee Abnormality Detection challenge asks teams to build multimodal models that estimate the probability of twelve clinically important findings per study, using imaging data paired with original radiology reports during training.

Decision statement: The proposed model will help radiologists and triage teams classify clinically important knee abnormalities by using multimodal knee MRI series and (training-time) radiology report text.

Why it matters

  • Single scans may contain multiple concurrent abnormalities.
  • Specialty expertise is unevenly distributed across sites.
  • Consistent triage can reduce delayed follow-up for high-risk findings.
  • Multimodal training (image + report) can improve label coverage where only a subset of studies is fully annotated.

Project objectives

Two to four measurable objectives (placeholders until research lock-in).

  1. Examine relationships between MRI study characteristics and the twelve labeled abnormalities.
  2. Develop a multilabel model that estimates per-finding probabilities for each knee MRI study.
  3. Translate model outputs into triage decision rules for radiologist review prioritization.

Project questions

  • Which imaging / derived features are most relevant to each finding?
  • What patterns appear across sites, scanners, and languages?
  • Which model family best supports multilabel detection under competition constraints?
  • How should probabilities map to practical triage actions with human oversight?
  • How will success be measured beyond leaderboard score?
