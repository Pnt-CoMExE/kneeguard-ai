Dataset & data model

Public competition dataset pairing knee MRI DICOM studies with multilingual radiology reports across twelve abnormality labels.

Dataset relevance

The dataset directly encodes the decision task — estimating twelve clinically important findings per study from real multi-site MRI exams. Training-time reports support weak labelling and multimodal learning. Inference-time reports are withheld, matching a realistic clinical deployment constraint.

Diversity across scanners, protocols, languages, and 19 sites strengthens external validity but also introduces domain shift between train, public, and private test splits.

Known limitations

  • Only a subset of training studies may carry complete per-condition labels.
  • Intensities, orientations, and resolutions vary across series.
  • Mixed transfer syntaxes; DICOM metadata is stripped to 86 allowlisted tags.
  • Expected class imbalance across rare findings (e.g., fracture, Baker's cyst).
  • Multilingual reports add complexity to text-based pipelines.
  • Prevalence may differ between train / public LB / private test sets.

Data quality & preprocessing plan

Issues to examine and planned handling.

Issue How to observe Planned handling
Missing labels Count unlabelled studies / findings per label Weak supervision from reports; mask unknown labels from supervised loss
Class imbalance Prevalence histogram per finding Class weights, focal loss, or stratified sampling
Intensity / protocol shift Scanner and site metadata summaries Per-series normalisation, series selection heuristics
Duplicates / data leakage Study ID uniqueness; patient-level deduplication Deduplicate; group-aware cross-validation splits
Sensitive information Review DICOM tags and report text for PHI Use competition-stripped metadata (86 allowlisted tags); avoid PHI export
Multilingual reports Language distribution across sites Multilingual encoder or a machine-translation pipeline
