Part 3 · Proposed decision model

Decision model design

Multilabel classification with optional multimodal training. Outputs become triage decisions under human oversight. Details marked PLACEHOLDER await teammate research.

Selected model type

Why multilabel classification over alternatives.

Primary: Multilabel classification model — imaging backbone with study-level aggregation and twelve independent sigmoid heads.
Optional hybrid: Report NLP / LLM for weak label extraction during training only (not used at inference).

Clustering does not produce calibrated finding probabilities. A single-output regression cannot express the twelve-label clinical structure evaluated by macro-AUC. Multilabel classification aligns directly with the task.

PLACEHOLDER — Final backbone (2.5D CNN, transformer, ensemble) and baseline comparison table from teammate experiments.

Candidate approaches

  • Baseline: per-finding mean-prevalence predictor (lower bound)
  • Candidate A: shared encoder + 12 sigmoid output heads
  • Candidate B: sequence-aware 2.5D / video-style backbone
  • Candidate C: multimodal image + text model (train time)
  • Efficiency track: latency / GPU runtime budget constraint

Model workflow

Ten-step flow from the course framework, adapted to this case.

1

Data collection

Obtain competition MRI studies, metadata, and training reports under license rules.

2

Data validation

Verify study IDs, series presence, label schema, and report text availability.

3

Cleaning & preprocessing

Normalise intensities, select informative sequences, and handle missing labels.

4

Feature engineering

Slice sampling strategy, image embeddings, and optional report-derived weak labels.

5

Model training

Train multilabel model with group-aware cross-validation folds across sites.

6

Validation & testing

Macro AUC-ROC, per-finding curves, and runtime checks for the efficiency track.

7

Result interpretation

Calibrate scores; inspect failure cases with domain expert review.

8

Decision recommendation

Map probabilities to Low / Medium / High triage priority bands.

9

User action

Radiologist reviews prioritised findings, confirms or overrides, and finalises the report.

10

Feedback & improvement

Log disagreements and outcomes; retrain or recalibrate on approved cohorts.

Evaluation & success criteria

  • Primary metric: macro-averaged AUC-ROC across all twelve findings
  • Secondary: per-finding AUC, precision / recall at selected operating points
  • Efficiency track: predictive performance vs GPU runtime (if pursued)
  • Decision-support usefulness rated by clinician partners
Draft success criterion: The model is considered successful if macro AUC-ROC ≥ TBD and at least 80% of reviewed triage recommendations are judged clinically useful by test reviewers.

Decision rules

Mapping model output to triage priority. Thresholds require clinical agreement.

Low
All critical findings < 0.40
Routine queue — standard reporting workflow
Medium
Any finding 0.40 – 0.69
Expedited review — notify responsible radiologist
High
Any critical finding ≥ 0.70
Immediate specialist review — document any override

Human confirmation is always required before any patient-facing clinical action.

Benefits, limitations, ethics & risks

Expected benefits

Faster triage, consistent screening across sites, support where MSK specialists are scarce, and structured second-read assistance for complex multilabel exams.

Limitations

Domain shift across scanners, incomplete labels, class imbalance, and possible over-reliance on report-derived weak supervision that may encode reporting style bias.

Ethics & risks

False negatives for fracture and ligament injury, fairness across sites and languages, imaging data privacy, limited model explainability, and continuous human oversight requirement.
